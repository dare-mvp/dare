# Make It A Dare - Content Series Strategy

## Purpose

This document defines the strategy, format rules, and execution plan for DARE's core recurring content series: **Make It A Dare**.

It is the primary content vehicle for building brand identity, driving organic reach, and demonstrating the product's core value proposition before and after app launch.

---

## Strategic Positioning

DARE occupies a specific brand lane:

> Accountability challenges for tasks, skills, proof, and friendly stakes.

This separates DARE from two failure modes:

- **Reckless internet challenges** - clout-chasing, unsafe, wrong audience, wrong behavior
- **Generic wagering apps** - anonymous, luck-based, no social layer, no skill proof

"Make It A Dare" owns the accountability lane. Every post reinforces that DARE is the platform for people who want to back their goals and abilities with real stakes, real proof, and real judges - not random outcomes.

This positioning is a brand moat. Competitors cannot copy it without rebuilding their product.

---

## Series Concept

**Make It A Dare** takes a real human struggle, unfinished goal, or friendly argument and converts it into a specific, executable DARE prescription.

The series runs daily. Volume and consistency are the strategy. Each post is short, concrete, and immediately applicable.

The format is:

```
[Real struggle, comment, or goal - one or two lines]

Make It A Dare:
Task: [specific, one-line challenge]
Proof: [exact proof method]
Deadline: [specific timeframe]
Stakes: [NGN/KES amount]
Judge: [who decides]
```

Specificity is what makes each post shareable. Vague prescriptions get ignored. Concrete ones get saved, shared to WhatsApp, and sent to the person the reader is thinking about.

---

## Format Rules

### The struggle line

- Source from real comments, tweets, WhatsApp group sentiment, or universal human experiences
- Keep it one or two lines maximum
- It must be instantly recognizable - the reader should think "this is literally me" or "this is literally my friend"
- Never fabricate struggles that are not relatable to a Nigerian or Kenyan adult aged 20-35

### The prescription

Each prescription must include all five fields. Do not publish a partial prescription.

**Task:** One specific, completable action. Not a habit. Not a vague goal. One deliverable with a clear end state.

**Proof:** The exact media or artifact that proves completion. Match the proof method to the task:
- Physical tasks: short video, same location each session
- Digital tasks: screen recording or screenshot of live result
- Creative tasks: the finished work posted publicly
- Social tasks: screenshot of the public post
- Knowledge tasks: video of the performance, no editing
- Money tasks: masked screenshot or in-app proof only; never ask people to expose account numbers, full balances, card details, or bank identifiers

**Deadline:** A specific timeframe, not "soon." Use hours or days. 48 hours, 7 days, 30 days. Match the deadline to the task difficulty.

**Stakes:** Keep the default range between NGN 500 and NGN 2,000, or the local Kenyan equivalent, for most posts. Higher stakes (up to NGN 5,000 or equivalent) are appropriate for longer or more demanding tasks. Stakes must feel real but not intimidating to a first-time user.

**Judge:** Always a human, not the platform. Name the judge archetype:
- A friend who has not seen the work
- The person the argument was with
- Three neutral people voting blind
- Someone with domain knowledge
- A trusted person selected by the participant, not a public pile-on

The human judge is central to DARE's product proposition. It is also what makes the prescription feel fair.

### Safety and trust guardrails

Every prescription must pass these checks before publishing:

- The task must be legal, non-harmful, and possible without public humiliation or harassment.
- The result must depend on effort, skill, completion, or judgment - never chance, odds, or random outcomes.
- The proof must not require sensitive personal data, full financial records, private messages, medical information, or identity documents.
- The judge must be named as a trusted person or small neutral group. Avoid open-ended public judging for private, financial, health, or body-related tasks.
- The stake must be framed as accountability, not gambling or income generation.

## Content Pillars

Run prescriptions across these categories in rotation. Do not post two prescriptions from the same pillar on consecutive days.

| Pillar | Core struggle | Example proof |
|--------|--------------|--------------|
| Skills and learning | Starting but quitting | Screen recording, live URL, video performance |
| Fitness and health | Promising to start, never starting | Daily video log, same location |
| Business and hustle | Idea with no execution | Screenshot of live post or first sale |
| Creative work | Unfinished project | Finished piece posted publicly |
| Financial discipline | Overspending, not saving | Masked savings proof on day 1 and day 30 |
| Relationship bets | Friendly arguments about ability | Blind judging, side-by-side proof |
| Language and knowledge | Learning something for years but not practicing | Timed video performance in the target skill |

## Platform Strategy

### Twitter / X

- Post the prescription as a standalone tweet
- Reply selectively to real tweets where the format is genuinely useful; avoid repetitive replies that look like spam
- Retweet and respond to people who pick up the format

### Instagram

- Single image or carousel with the prescription text
- Caption repeats the full format
- Story version: animated text card, swipe to see the prescription
- Add the CTA on post 4 onward: "Drop your goal in the comments and we'll Make It A Dare"

### TikTok

- 15 to 30 second voiceover reading the struggle and prescription
- Text on screen matches the spoken words
- The 2-person UGC video format (see Video Format section below) is the premium version for strongest prescriptions

### WhatsApp

- Weekly digest of that week's best prescriptions shared to the DARE WhatsApp channel
- Community members can submit their own struggles via WhatsApp for the team to prescribe

## Community Sourcing

After the first three to four posts establish the format, add this CTA to every post:

> Drop your goal, your unfinished project, or your friendly argument in the comments. We'll Make It A Dare.

This converts the audience into the content pipeline. It also provides:

- Continuous proof that real people have these struggles
- Content that the person who submitted it will share because it is theirs
- Data on which categories resonate most by comment volume

Prescriptions written from community submissions should tag the person who submitted where platform policy allows.

## What Not To Do

**Do not post reckless or unsafe challenges.** No eating challenges, no physical stunts without clear safety framing, no social pressure on strangers. These attract wrong behavior and the wrong audience.

**Do not make prescriptions vague.** "Exercise more" is not a prescription. "Complete 7 consecutive days of 20-minute morning exercise, video proof, same location each day" is a prescription. Vague posts get ignored. Specific posts get shared.

**Do not use the series to hard-sell the app.** The CTA is always soft - "DARE makes this real" or "This is exactly what DARE was built for." Let the product logic sell itself. The series earns credibility by being genuinely useful, not by being an ad.

**Do not turn private proof into public exposure.** Financial, health, relationship, and identity-related proof should be masked, limited to the judge, or handled inside the app. The series should normalize trust and privacy from the beginning.

**Do not post two prescriptions from the same pillar on consecutive days.** Rotate the categories to keep the feed fresh and avoid appearing niche.

## Success Signals

Track these per post:

- Saves and shares (strongest signal - people are bookmarking and sending to friends)
- Comments that name a specific friend or submit a new struggle
- Replies that adopt the format organically
- WhatsApp forwards (harder to track, but visible through referral link patterns)
- Profile visits after each post

Do not optimize for likes alone. Saves and shares indicate intent, not just passive approval.
